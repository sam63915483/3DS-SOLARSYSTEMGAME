Orbit — gimbal instrument mockups

Top screen is 400×240, shown at 3×. Instrument lives in a 90×90 box at (4,146) — 4 px from the left and bottom edges. Every mark is a filled rect, filled triangle, filled circle, or 5×7 capital font pixel; each design is one gimbalX(ctx, x, y, state) function below so it can be moved to citro2d nearly line by line. Palette: FFC040 accent F0F0F0 off-white 9098A8 grey-blue 20243A panel 3C7CC8 sky (A,B) 4E8A3A planet (A,B) 60E878 phosphor (C)

A — Navball / artificial horizon

A 60 px ball split into a sky-blue upper half and a planet-green lower half. The horizon chord rotates with roll and slides up as you pitch toward the planet (a wholly green ball means the planet is dead ahead, wholly blue means it is behind you); two faint ticks either side of the horizon give a sense of pitch, and a small white notch on the rim turns with yaw. A fixed orange "wings" reticle marks you. Jets sit around the ball where they act: UP is the triangle above, DOWN the triangle below, the two roll arrows ride the shoulders of the ball pointing the way you will turn, FORWARD lights the centre dot of the reticle, and FWD / BRAKE are lit key-caps along the bottom. Reads like a flight sim: green on the bottom means you are upright, more green means nose down.

Build cost: 3 circles, a 12-triangle fan for the planet segment, 1 quad (2 triangles) for the horizon, 5 small triangles, ~10 rects, two 5x7 labels.

B — Astronaut and planet dot, with a thrust key column

A 64 px square viewport that always shows you: an upright blocky astronaut seen from behind. The planet is a green dot drawn where it sits in your view — below you when level, sliding round the frame as you roll, drifting toward the centre and growing as you pitch to face it (at the centre and largest it is dead ahead; if it is behind you it becomes a hollow ring). A short sky-blue bar through the dot is the planet's horizon, so roll is obvious even at a glance. Jets show twice: as orange flames on the figure itself (under the boots for UP, over the helmet for DOWN, out of the hands for roll, a glowing backpack for FORWARD) and as a column of six key-caps down the right edge that invert to orange when lit. A tiny grey pitch/roll readout sits under the frame for the numerically minded. Reads like a character portrait: you see your little astronaut and where the ground is.

Build cost: ~14 rects (frame, figure, key-caps), 1–2 circles, up to 4 flame triangles, 1 quad for the horizon bar, 6 labels + 1 readout string.

C — Retro radar scope with a six-segment jet ring

A dark bezel with a crosshair, tick marks and a thin "level" ring. A phosphor-green needle and dot show where the planet is as a polar plot: the direction of the needle is the direction of "down" in your view (straight down the screen when upright, swinging round as you roll), and its length is how far you are from facing the planet — the dot rides exactly on the ring when you are level, shrinks to the centre as you pitch down to look at the planet, and pushes outside the ring when the planet is behind you (drawn hollow). Around the outside, six trapezoid segments light orange for each jet, each with a two- or three-letter label beside it: UP top, DN bottom, RL and RR on the upper diagonals, BRK and FWD on the lower ones. Reads like an old radar or an early Elite-style scanner: the dot on the ring and nothing lit means you are hanging level and coasting.

Build cost: 2 rects (bezel), 2 circles (ring), ~6 rects (crosshair/ticks), 6 quads (12 triangles), 1 quad for the needle, 2 circles for the dot, 6 labels.
